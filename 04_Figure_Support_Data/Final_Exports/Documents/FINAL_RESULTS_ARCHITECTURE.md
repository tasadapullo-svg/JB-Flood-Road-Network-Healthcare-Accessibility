# Frozen Results Architecture — Scientific Reports

## 3.1 Historical flood geography and recurrent flood zones
**Purpose:** establish where repeatedly observed flooding occurs in Johor Bahru.

Report:
- F01–F19 as the frozen historical evidence-support layer.
- 9 Severe, 8 Recurrent and 2 Mild zones.
- Two locations observed in all three core years (2022, 2023 and 2025).
- Geometry confidence and the four LOW-confidence grid-supported proxies.
- Explicitly state that the polygons are not a complete hydraulic inundation footprint.

Primary outputs: Fig. 1A–B; Table 2; Table S1.

## 3.2 Physical context of the historical flood zones
**Purpose:** describe whether the observed geography is physically plausible without redefining the zones.

Report:
- elevation, slope, relative elevation and strict low-lying terrain as descriptive context;
- no susceptibility-model fitting;
- absence of a strict low-lying pixel does not invalidate historical evidence.

Primary outputs: Fig. 1C; Table 2.

## 3.3 Correspondence with observed road disruption
**Purpose:** connect historical flood geography with documented operational transport consequences.

Report:
- 8/8 road-type frozen zones have same-road 2023 observed impacts;
- Jalan Kolam Ayer has explicit closure correspondence;
- Gertak Merah remains source-limited and is not counted as an exact closure-zone match;
- 2025 spatially authorized disruptions do not directly intersect F01–F19, demonstrating that the frozen historical layer is localized and non-exhaustive.

Primary outputs: Fig. 3; Tables S2–S3.

## 3.4 Healthcare-accessibility impact of the 20 March 2025 disruption
**Purpose:** quantify the network consequence of the observed FT003 + FT01 + J105 disruption.

Report:
- 544 newly unreachable origins;
- 4,961.045 newly unreachable people (0.260319% of total);
- 30-min population coverage decline of 0.260319 percentage points;
- the corrected baseline unreachable stock increases from about 5,154 to 10,116;
- common-reachable mean travel-time increase is only about 0.009 min.

Interpretation:
the dominant effect is localized loss of network reachability rather than large citywide average delay.

Primary outputs: Fig. 2A–B; Fig. 4A–B; Table 3.

## 3.5 Spatial inequality of healthcare-accessibility loss
**Purpose:** identify where the accessibility loss is concentrated.

Report:
- Mukim Tebrau: 4,682.941 newly unreachable people, 94.394% of the total newly unreachable population;
- Mukim Plentong: 278.104 newly unreachable people;
- other analyzed subdistricts show no newly unreachable population under the nominal primary event.

Primary outputs: Fig. 2B; Fig. 4C; Table 3.

## 3.6 Sensitivity, road criticality and recovery priority
**Purpose:** distinguish robust conclusions from location-sensitive magnitudes and identify practical restoration priorities.

Report:
- 27 spatializations yield 2,553.735–5,079.075 newly unreachable people;
- J105 has the largest nominal single-road effect but is location-sensitive;
- FT01 shows stable high impact;
- FT003 has a smaller residual connectivity/rerouting role;
- J46 has zero modeled healthcare-accessibility loss and must remain visible as a negative result;
- nominal best sequence: J105 → FT01 → FT003;
- J105 + FT01 recover 90.682% of the nominal initial loss after two restoration steps;
- operationally freeze J105 and FT01 as the principal Tier-1 recovery-critical group.

Primary outputs: Fig. 2C; Fig. 5; Table 4; Tables S4–S5.
