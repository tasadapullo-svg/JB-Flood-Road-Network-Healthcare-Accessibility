and the loss remains concentrated in Mukim Tebrau throughout all 27 closure-location combinations.

6. GIS FEASIBILITY
YES — the existing evidence can already be represented in GIS at three scientifically distinct levels:

A_OBSERVED_ROAD_DISRUPTION:
road sections with direct documented 2025 closure/disruption geometry.

B_HIGH_EVIDENCE_SUSCEPTIBILITY:
roads supported by recurrent/official hotspot evidence plus terrain low-lying support,
or by overlapping official and historical recurrent screening evidence.

C_POTENTIAL_SUSCEPTIBILITY:
roads supported by at least one of:
official hotspot proximity,
historical recurrent-location proximity,
or strong terrain low-lying evidence.

The package also includes:
- 31 spatially verified official recurrent hotspot points;
- exact historical recurrent locations;
- 250 m screening zones;
- the D03 strict low-lying terrain raster;
- all D03 road segments with screening class.

7. IMPORTANT GIS LIMIT
The B/C classes are evidence-based SUSCEPTIBILITY SCREENING classes.
They are NOT calibrated annual flood probabilities.

Do NOT label them:
'80% flood probability',
'high probability of flooding',
or a return-period probability.

Safe map terminology:
Observed recurrent flood hotspot
Observed flood-disrupted road
High-evidence flood susceptibility
Potential flood susceptibility
Terrain low-lying screening

To claim quantitative flood probability, a later hazard model must be fitted and validated
using repeated event/non-event samples, rainfall frequency, drainage/river/tide conditions,
and preferably inundation/depth observations.
