# Step 7 — Flood-related Road Disruption → Healthcare Accessibility Impact v1.0

## Frozen event design
- 2025 is the primary quantitative healthcare-accessibility event.
- 20 March 2025: FT003 + FT01 + J105 form the temporally coherent primary disruption scenario.
- 21 March 2025: J46 is retained as an observed negative/limited-impact case.
- 2023 remains secondary operational validation only; it is not forced into quantitative network simulation.

## Primary result
The 20 March scenario produced:
- 544 newly unreachable origins.
- 4961.045 newly unreachable people.
- 0.260319% of total population newly unreachable.
- 30-min population coverage change: -0.260319 percentage points.

The citywide mean travel-time increase among origins reachable in both baseline and disruption scenarios was only
0.009188 min
(0.127423%).

The important resilience effect is therefore loss of network reachability, not a large citywide average travel-time increase.

## Network-isolation interpretation
Corrected baseline unreachable population: 5154.484.
Event unreachable population: 10115.529.
The flood-related disruptions added 4961.045 unreachable people,
equivalent to a 96.247% increase relative to the corrected baseline unreachable stock.

## Spatial concentration
Mukim Tebrau:
- 4682.941 newly unreachable.
- 0.970675% of Tebrau population.
- 94.394% of all newly unreachable people.

Mukim Plentong:
- 278.104 newly unreachable.
- 0.045863% of Plentong population.

The consequence is highly localized.

## Single-road attribution
Nominal newly unreachable population:
- J105: 3219.757
- FT01: 1278.999
- FT003: 462.288
- J46: 0.000

J105 has the largest nominal impact, but its exact magnitude is location-sensitive.
FT01 is a stable Tier-1 corridor across its declared ±50 m spatial uncertainty.
FT003 has a smaller but persistent connectivity/rerouting role.
J46 has no measurable healthcare-accessibility effect in this model.

## Sensitivity
Across all 27 spatializations of the 20 March closures:
- newly unreachable population: 2553.735 to 5079.075
- median: 4961.045
- share of total population: 0.134001% to 0.266512%

Mukim Tebrau remains the dominant spatial concentration:
- newly unreachable: 2275.631 to 4691.305
- share of all newly unreachable: 85.441% to 94.404%

Therefore the qualitative core finding is robust, but the exact effect size is not universal.

## Recovery
Nominal best sequence:
J105 → FT01 → FT003

After the first road:
- 3219.757 people restored
- 64.901% of the initial accessibility loss restored

After two roads:
- 4498.757 people restored
- 90.682% restored

Because J105 is spatially sensitive, the robust operational conclusion is:
J105 and FT01 form the principal Tier-1 recovery-critical corridor group.
Do not claim that J105 is uniquely dominant in every spatialization.
