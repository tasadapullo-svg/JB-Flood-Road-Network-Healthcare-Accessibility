# Frozen Discussion Logic

## D1. From recurrent flood evidence to transport consequence
The study's first contribution is not a new flood-prediction model. It is an evidence-controlled spatial framework that converts multi-year historical flood reporting into closed, auditable GIS zones and then links those zones to observed transport consequences.

The 2023 same-road correspondence shows that the historical geography is operationally meaningful. The 2025 non-overlap result is equally important: the frozen zones are localized recurrent evidence areas, not an exhaustive citywide hazard surface.

## D2. Connectivity loss is more informative than citywide mean delay
The 20 March 2025 event creates a small change in average travel time among origins that remain reachable, yet 4,961 people become newly unreachable. A mean-delay-only evaluation would therefore understate the resilience consequence.

The discussion should emphasize network isolation, reachability loss and destination access rather than only average travel-time deterioration.

## D3. Spatial inequality is concentrated
About 94.4% of the newly unreachable population is located in Mukim Tebrau. This demonstrates unequal spatial exposure to the network consequence.

Use "spatial inequality" or "uneven accessibility impact." Avoid stronger claims about socioeconomic inequity unless additional socioeconomic variables are analyzed.

## D4. Criticality depends on network position, not simply flood occurrence
J105 and FT01 produce far larger healthcare-accessibility effects than J46. This demonstrates that an observed road disruption and its system consequence are not equivalent.

Road criticality should therefore be interpreted through the interaction of disruption location, network topology, population distribution and hospital destinations.

## D5. Recovery priority
J105 has the largest nominal benefit, while FT01 is highly stable under its location uncertainty. The operationally defensible conclusion is that J105 and FT01 form the Tier-1 recovery-critical group.

The nominal J105 → FT01 → FT003 sequence is useful for scenario interpretation, but should not be presented as a universally optimal emergency-management rule.

## D6. Methodological limits
The frozen flood polygons represent historical evidence-support areas rather than hydraulic inundation boundaries.
Four zones use LOW-confidence grid-supported proxy geometry.
2023 Gertak Merah exact affected edges remain unresolved.
The 2025 accessibility experiment is event-specific and based on reference routing assumptions.
D09 is retained only as preparedness screening because historical evidence is embedded in its construction.
These limits should be stated without undermining the supported results.
