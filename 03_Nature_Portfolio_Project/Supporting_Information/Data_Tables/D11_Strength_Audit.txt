D11 SCIENTIFIC-RESULTS STRENGTH AUDIT
Version: v1.0

OVERALL JUDGMENT
RESULT STRUCTURE: SUFFICIENTLY STRONG FOR A COHERENT SCIENTIFIC REPORTS MANUSCRIPT,
provided the paper is framed as an event-based urban flood–road–healthcare-accessibility resilience study,
not as a probabilistic flood-hazard model.

WHY THE RESULTS ARE STRONG
1. The evidence chain is complete:
   rainfall / flood evidence
   -> documented road disruption
   -> healthcare-network disconnection
   -> spatial concentration
   -> critical-road preparedness
   -> quantified recovery benefit.

2. The main accessibility result is not dependent on a single closure geometry:
   newly unreachable range = 2554–5079 people.

3. Spatial inequality is highly consistent:
   Tebrau share = 85.4–94.4% of newly unreachable across sensitivity.

4. Recovery benefits are directly quantified:
   J105-first nominally restores 64.9% of initial loss.
   J105 + FT01 restore 90.7%.

5. Negative/limited findings are retained:
   - citywide mean delay is very small;
   - J46 has negligible healthcare-accessibility consequence;
   - J105 is not uniquely first under all spatial uncertainty;
   - CRPI does not equal flood probability.

WHAT THE PAPER MUST NOT BECOME
- A claim of citywide travel-time collapse.
- A claim of statistically significant independent observations based on 75,149 population cells.
- A calibrated flood-probability map.
- A general causal statement that rainfall directly caused every closure at an exact timestamp.

MAIN REMAINING SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS
1. Quantitative accessibility/recovery results are anchored to one major March-2025 event.
2. Exact road-closure onset/clearance timestamps are unavailable.
3. Two observed context-road closures lie outside the D01 district core but influence the routing context.
4. The susceptibility layer is evidence-based screening, not annual probability.
5. FCD is not used to calibrate event-specific flood speeds across the entire road network.

Recommended final main text:
- 5 core figures.
- 4 core tables.
This is enough. Do not add another major result family unless peer-review or a clear research question requires it.
